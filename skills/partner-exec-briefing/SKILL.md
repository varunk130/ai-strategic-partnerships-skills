# Partner Exec Briefing

Send your executive into a partner meeting knowing exactly what to ask for, what to avoid, and who follows up.

## When to Use

- An executive sponsor is meeting their counterpart at a partner
- A stalled partnership needs an exec-level unblock
- Annual exec sync, summit side meeting, or escalation call
- A new executive inherits a partner relationship

## What You'll Need

**Critical inputs (ask if not provided):**

- Partner name (synthetic in examples, e.g. "Kestrel AI"), attendees on both sides, date and length
- The one outcome we want from this meeting
- Current relationship state: what is working, what is stuck

**Nice-to-have:**

- Latest QBR or joint business plan numbers
- Partner's recent public statements, earnings themes, or reorgs
- Notes from the last exec meeting and open commitments

## Process

### Step 1: Diagnose the relationship state

Give the executive one honest line, not a status report.

| State | Signal | Executive posture |
|-------|--------|-------------------|
| Accelerating | Pipeline up, commitments met | Expand: propose the next bet |
| Steady | Delivering, no momentum | Re-energize: name one new joint goal |
| Stalled | Missed commitments, slow replies | Unblock: name the blocker, ask for an owner |
| Strained | Escalations, channel conflict | Repair: acknowledge, agree a fix, avoid new asks |
| New | No history | Align: agree why, test sponsorship |

### Step 2: Map their strategy and pressures

What their executive is measured on this year determines what they will say yes to. List two or three pressures (growth target, AI roadmap, margin, board narrative) and how this partnership helps or hurts each.

### Step 3: Set wants on both sides

| | We want | They likely want |
|-|---------|------------------|
| From this meeting | | |
| This quarter | | |
| Where we conflict | | |

### Step 4: Write three talking points

Each one: a point, the proof, and the bridge to the ask. Three, not seven. The executive will remember three.

### Step 5: Flag landmines

Topics that cost trust if raised wrong: an open escalation, a competing partner we also work with, a deal we lost together, a recent layoff, pricing disputes. For each, say what to do if they raise it.

### Step 6: Make the ask specific and assign follow-up

An ask has a verb, an owner on their side, and a date. "Alignment on AI" is not an ask. "Assign a product lead to the agent integration by March 15" is. Name our follow-up owner and the 48-hour deadline for the recap.

## Output Format

```markdown
# Exec Brief: [Our exec] x [Their exec], [Partner]
[Date] | [Length] | Attendees: [names, roles]

**Relationship state:** [State]: [one line why]
**Meeting goal:** [one outcome]

## Their strategy and pressures
- [Pressure]: [how we help or hurt]

## Wants
| | Us | Them |

## Talking points
1. [Point]: [proof] -> [bridge to ask]
2.
3.

## Landmines
- [Topic]: if raised, [response]

## The ask
[Verb] [what] by [date], owned by [their name]

## Follow-up
Owner: [name] | Recap sent by: [date, within 48h]
```

Post-meeting capture (fill within 24 hours):

```markdown
## Exec Meeting Capture: [Partner], [date]
- Ask outcome: [yes / no / deferred] - [detail]
- Commitments made by us: [item, owner, date]
- Commitments made by them: [item, owner, date]
- New information: [strategy shifts, people changes]
- Relationship state now: [state], changed because [reason]
- Next exec touchpoint: [date]
```

## Guardrails

- One page. If the executive will not read it in five minutes, cut it.
- Do not speculate about the partner's internal politics as fact; mark inference as inference.
- Never include confidential information from another partner.
- Do not put an ask in the brief that the deal team has not cleared.
